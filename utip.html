Product lens

Revisit through discovery

Shipping a working product and creating a valuable product are not necessarily the same thing.

I wanted to revisit uTip by starting somewhere different — not with features, but with the user problem.

The Problem

The calculator worked. The uncertainty didn’t go away.

question first →

The bigger question wasn’t “can people calculate a tip?” — it was whether the product helped with the moments of friction people actually feel when deciding what to tip.

Assumption To be validated

People may experience friction beyond arithmetic — choosing a percentage, splitting context, rounding the final amount, or wanting a fast, distraction-free path.

Evidence In progress

No validated research findings yet. User interviews and feedback are planned so assumptions can be tested — not treated as fact.

Reflection

What I’m Learning About Product

Working principles for this case study. They’re not claims that research has already proven specific product outcomes.

  • Assumptions are not evidence.

    A plausible idea still needs validation before it drives roadmap decisions.

  • A working feature is not necessarily a valuable feature.

    Shipping proves feasibility. Value requires understanding whether it reduced real friction.

  • Discovery should influence what gets built.

    The next version of uTip should be shaped by what users struggle with — not by what is easy to add.

  • Simplicity can be a product decision.

    Saying no protects the core job when extra options would slow people down.

  • Prioritization requires saying no.

    Tradeoffs are clearer when “Not pursuing” is an explicit option.
